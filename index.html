Virun Yatra pilot pitch, for tomorrow

Pilot pitch, not a launch announcement

Trips planned by AI, guided by verified locals.

A tourist in an unknown city should not have to trust a random blog, a generic AI answer, or a stranger at a station. Virun pairs an AI trip plan with a local guide who speaks the tourist's language, knows the place, and stays available during the trip.

The ask, in one line

Do not launch Virun alone. Launch it as a pilot with Yatra.

Alone, a new name has no trust. No tourist trusts an unknown startup, and no local trusts an unknown platform with their time and income. With Yatra, the same product gets trust, reach, and publicity from day one. That is the whole point of this pitch: Yatra builds and launches it with us, not just funds it.

Presented by P V N Dhanunjay Reddy. This is a partnership pitch, not a hackathon entry, so no team slide is included.

1. Problem

Travelers in an unfamiliar city struggle to find trustworthy places to eat, stay, and visit, choose affordable transport, and understand local culture. Online content is often generic, sponsored, or outdated. Language barriers and tourist scams add risk. Solo travelers can feel uncomfortable exploring alone.

What the survey answers kept repeating, in their own words: not knowing where to go, last minute hotel problems, transport stops that Google does not explain, food that does not match, and timing that falls apart. A plan alone does not fix this. A trusted local, available during the trip, does.

How people plan today

54% plan the trip themselves with no external help. 39% prefer unplanned adventure. For tools, Google was the most common answer, followed by ChatGPT, then travel agencies. No single tool gives a plan plus a trusted local during the trip. That gap is Virun.

What people will pay for a guide, 4 to 6 days, food and transport excluded

Online guide: Rs 200 to 400: 27%. Rs 400 to 600: 36%. Rs 600 to 800: 22%. Rs 800 to 1000: 16%.

63% are in the Rs 200 to 600 range for online help. 84% are at or below Rs 800.

Offline, in person guide: Rs 1000 to 1500: 33%. Rs 1500 to 2000: 28%. Rs 2000 to 2500: 28%. Rs 2500 to 3000: 12%.

88% are in the Rs 1000 to 2500 range for an in person guide.

The hard truth about guide supply

Interest is broad, firm commitment is small. Asked if they would be a paid guide, 23% said yes and 33% said they do not mind, so 55% are open. But in the follow up question, answered by only 39 people, only 6 said yes firmly to working as a guide on the platform, while 33 said no, they just wanted to explore.

This is why the Yatra pilot matters more than the app. Supply will not appear because a website exists. It will appear because a trusted brand recruits, verifies, and publicises it. Do not hide this number from Yatra. It is the reason to partner.

4. What is built, and what is not

Almost done: the planner prototype

From the Virun repo at github.com/KinGMssN/virun, checked 31 October 2026:

  • Static frontend plus FastAPI backend, with route planning, trip recovery, transport listing, and natural language trip parsing.
  • AI stay suggestions and place lookup, with real OpenStreetMap place names where available.
  • Train schedule lookup, with fares and booking clearly not supplied by that provider.
  • Demo destinations in the repo: Kashmir, Goa, Hyderabad, Visakhapatnam, Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru.
  • Supabase sign in integrated in code, but live verification is still pending.

This is a demo and planner, not a launched product.

Not done, to build with Yatra

  • Planned Guide verification and KYC, with roles enforced on the server.
  • Planned Booking, with confirmation, cancellation, and status sync.
  • Planned Payment and escrow, with a real payment processor, refunds, and verified webhooks.
  • Planned Live bus schedules, fares, and inventory from a contracted provider.
  • Planned Production deployment, monitoring, backups, support contact, and data retention rules.
  • Planned Guide discovery, trip chat, trip tracking, and SOS as live services. In the repo today these are sample or preview flows only.

Do not show Yatra a KYC, escrow, or SOS screen as if it is live. Show it as the next build, with Yatra.

Tech stack decision: to be made together with the Yatra team. This pitch does not ask Yatra to accept a solo stack. The goal is a real working prototype built by a collaborating team, not a solo build presented as finished.

5. How Virun would work, in the pilot

  1. Tourist chooses destination, language, budget, and interests. AI creates a first trip plan.
  2. Tourist matches a verified local guide by language, place knowledge, and online or in person mode.
  3. Guide reviews and personalises the plan, with food, transport, hidden places, and local experience.
  4. During the trip, the guide stays available through the platform, with contact details protected.
  5. Payment, safety, and reviews run through the platform, once the Yatra built payment and verification work is live.

7. Feasibility: pilot first, then scale

  1. Pilot: Visakhapatnam and Chennai. Recruit and verify a first set of guides, launch with Yatra listing and publicity, and measure real bookings, repeat use, guide retention, and complaints.
  2. India: expand city by city only where guide supply and verification hold up. Do not expand on bookings alone.
  3. International: only after the India pilot proves guide quality, safety, and payment at scale.

Note: Visakhapatnam is already a demo destination in the repo. Chennai is a pilot city for tomorrow, not yet a demo destination in the repo. Say so if asked.

Pilot success measures: verified guides onboarded, first bookings completed, guide repeat rate, tourist repeat or referral rate, safety issues and resolution time, and guide income per trip. Set targets with Yatra after the first guide interviews, not before.

8. Revenue: proposed, not fixed

The founder is clear on this point: the take rate is not fixed yet, and should be agreed with Yatra. The survey gives the demand signal, not the price.

  • Trip plans: pay per trip for occasional travelers.
  • Subscription: for frequent travelers, with no extra trip plan charge beyond the guide fee.
  • Partner income: collaborations with local restaurants and hotels, from the business plan document.
  • Guide commission: to be set with Yatra, after pilot costs and guide income are known. Earlier pitch drafts used 15 to 20% as an illustration only. Do not present that as a final price.

From the business plan document, a lean MVP was estimated at Rs 10 lakh, with break even in 18 months. For Yatra, the ask here is different: partnership and a joint build, not a Rs 10 lakh solo funding request. If Yatra wants a cost plan, build it together after the pilot scope is agreed.
